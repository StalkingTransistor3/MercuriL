MercuriL

The map that sees the flood.

Navigation apps route drivers onto flooded roads because no data source tells them the road is gone. Australia's national closure feed is a daily harmonisation of state open data — built for planned roadworks, not for the crossing that went under an hour ago.

87% of fatal vehicle flood incidents happen at crossings — causeways, bridges, dips. Nobody is standing there at 2am to report them.

MercuriL is a network of low-cost satellite-IoT depth sensors at exactly those crossings, feeding verified, real-time closures into the maps people already trust.

What the official feed can and can't tell you

Australia has a national road-closure dataset, harmonised from state open data. These are its own numbers for flood-relevant NSW records that present as currently in force — computed live, from dates in the source, at every page load.

This is not negligence, and the councils and agencies filing these records are not the problem. It is what happens when the only record of a flooded crossing is a form somebody fills in during an emergency, at night, in the rain — and there is no instrument anywhere that closes it out when the water goes down.

So the dataset does the honest thing and leaves the record open. Which means it can tell you a crossing went under. It cannot tell you whether it is under right now. Nothing in it is measuring the water. That is the gap.
