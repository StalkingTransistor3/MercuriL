MercuriL

Navigation apps route drivers onto flooded roads because no data source tells them the road is gone. Australia's national closure feed is a daily harmonisation of state open data, built for planned roadworks, not for the crossing that went under an hour ago.

MercuriL is a prototype network of depth and velocity instruments with satellite backhaul. Reports from provisioned field units can automatically flag closures on this map and trigger a route around them.

What the official feed can and can't tell you

Australia has a national road-closure dataset, harmonised from state open data. These are its own numbers for flood-relevant NSW records that present as currently in force. Counts are computed live from source dates at every page load.

This is not negligence, and the councils and agencies filing these records are not the problem. It is what happens when the only record of a flooded crossing is a form somebody fills in during an emergency, at night, in the rain, and there is no instrument anywhere that closes it out when the water goes down.

So the dataset does the honest thing and leaves the record open. Which means it can tell you a crossing went under. It cannot tell you whether it is under right now. Nothing in it is measuring the water. That is the gap.

What's real in this prototype

  • Real: grey dots show NSW closures/hazards from the federal Roadworks & Road Closures dataset (refreshed daily). Routes use the real road network.
  • Real instrument closures: red double-ring pins from installed MercuriL units. Click for the triggering report, timestamp and D×V. These are instrument warnings, separate from government closure records.
  • Bench units: real hardware at a labelled display position, with install coordinates pending. Bench reports do not close roads or affect routes.
  • Simulated: brass-outline pins and devices labelled simulated. Their demonstration floods affect routes in “With MercuriL”; every route alert retains that label.
  • Today: a comparison route with sensor avoidance disabled. It does not reproduce another navigation app's decisions.

How closure warnings work

Device WARNING or CLOSED reports hold a crossing closed. The server applies a conservative small-passenger-vehicle screen: depth ≥ 0.30 m, water speed ≥ 3.0 m/s, or depth × speed ≥ 0.30 m²/s closes the crossing. Only a fresh, newer positive OPEN report with complete measurements below all three limits reopens it; missing, blind or overdue reports hold the closure. A neutral pin can mean unknown conditions.

Satellite reports can be hourly. Each popup shows the sample age and configured cadence. The 15-minute stale flag remains visible; it never clears a closure.

The pilot trigger draws on the small-passenger-vehicle criteria discussed in Smith, Modra, Tucker & Cox (2017), UNSW WRL TR 2017/07, Table 7-2 and Figure 7-3. MercuriL closes at the limits as a conservative operating choice. Readings below them do not verify road integrity or give permission to enter floodwater; calibrated depth above the road and credible water velocity are required.
